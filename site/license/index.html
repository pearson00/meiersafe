License: copy, adapt and share, with credit

In brief

Mike Meier's own text, diagrams and photos on meiersafe.com are licensed Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International (CC BY-SA 4.0). You may copy, adapt and share them, including in paid courses, as long as you credit the source and share adaptations under the same license. Material by other people is not covered, and is credited where it appears.

What you may do #

  • Share: copy and pass on the material, in any medium or format, for any purpose, including commercially.
  • Adapt: edit, translate, cut, combine and build on it, for any purpose, including commercially.

A safety officer can print a page for a stand-down, a test-pilot school can put the framework in its course notes, and anyone can translate it. These freedoms can't be revoked as long as you follow the license terms.

What you must do #

  • Credit the source: name the title and Mike Meier, link to the page and to the license, and say if you made changes. Do it in any reasonable way, but not in a way that suggests Mike endorses you or your use.
  • Share alike: if you adapt the material, share your version under the same license.
  • Add no restrictions: don't apply legal terms or technical measures that stop others doing what the license allows.

A credit line to copy #

Every page ends with its own credit line: title, author, address and license. For one page, copy that line, for example:

Murphy's Corollary by Mike Meier, meiersafe.com/murphys-corollary/, CC BY-SA 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

For a version you have changed:

Adapted from Murphy's Corollary by Mike Meier, meiersafe.com/murphys-corollary/, CC BY-SA 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). Changes: [say what you changed].

For the framework as a whole, as in the Briefing Kit:

Lessons Learned and Murphy's Corollary by Mike Meier, meiersafe.com, licensed CC BY-SA 4.0.

On a web page, link the title to the page, Mike's name to About, and "CC BY-SA 4.0" to the license. To cite a page in a paper rather than reuse it, use the "Cite this page" block on that page.

The meiersafe.com edition is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0, with a note of the original copyright: the paper as a PDF.

Mike's photos #

Mike's own photos are licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 like his text, so trainers can use them in briefings: the test-flying photos on About and the photo of the correctly connected sweep wire on Hard questions.

What the license does not cover #

The license covers Mike's own text, diagrams and photos. It does not cover the following, each credited where it appears. To reuse any of these, go to the source.

  • Quotations from other people. Their words are theirs: for example Gene Kranz, Larry Mulloy, Rod Huete and Doug Wickert on Three Random Thoughts On Safety, and Alan Shepard and Deke Slayton's Moonshot on The pressure to go.
  • Art Tomassetti's chart and words. His skill-and-luck chart, from the May 2021 Flight Test Safety Fact, and the words quoted from him are his. Mike's charts on Luck and uncertainty adapt Art's chart, so they credit him and are outside the license too. In October 2026 Art told Mike he had "no issues with any of it," so the charts may be shown in a briefing with credit to both; for any other use, ask Art and Mike.
  • Quotations from investigation reports. Quotations from investigation reports, such as the Rogers Commission's report on Challenger, are U.S. government works, not Mike's to license; credit the source when you use them. Case studies.
  • Photos and video that aren't Mike's. The landing-crash video in the Anaheim talk is Dave Aldrich's, used with his permission. The cord-and-tang photo is of another pilot's glider; its photographer is unknown, and it is used with the permission of Davis Straub, who published it in the Oz Report. The flight clips in Mike's talks are his own footage, except the upwind landing of the Fledge, which comes from one of Tom Tatum's films.
  • The "Aviation in itself is not inherently dangerous" poster. Its text is quoted; the image is not used. Hard questions.
  • The SETP recordings. SETP recorded the Anaheim talk and made the Fellows induction video, and owns both; they appear here with SETP's permission.

Mike's "Lucky or Good – Further Thoughts" first ran in Flight Test Safety Fact in September 2021. Mark Jones, who published it there, is fine with Mike republishing it here.
