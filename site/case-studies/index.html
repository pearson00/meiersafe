Case studies: long runs of success, then failure

Key idea

In each of the three NASA cases, the investigation found a problem that should have been recognized and corrected, and was not. Each case page tells what happened, then reads it against the three factors, and ends with questions for your own program.

The cases #

  1. The 1995 top landing Hang gliding · July 1995

    Some 1,500 landings on the hilltop over fifteen years, each a success, then a hard crash after a flawless approach. The case where Murphy's Corollary was first recognized.

  2. The 2015 certification launch Hang gliding · February 3, 2015

    A deadline, pilots depending on him, and a wind that wasn't going to improve. Mike went, seven weeks before presenting the paper.

    From the 2018 Gulfstream talk; not in the paper.

  3. Apollo 1 Spaceflight · 1967

    We'd had a history of success with a pressurized pure-oxygen environment in manned capsules, and even previous fires in such environments. The Senate committee concluded that NASA's long history of successes with pure oxygen "led to overconfidence and complacency."

  4. Challenger Spaceflight · 1986

    Shuttles flew successfully with O-ring erosion and hot-gas blow-by in the booster joints. Influenced by repeated successes, NASA and Thiokol expanded both the blow-by they accepted and the low temperatures they would launch in. Feynman called it "a kind of Russian roulette."

  5. Columbia Spaceflight · 2003

    Shuttles re-entered and landed with heat-shield tiles damaged by foam shed from the external tank. "With each successful landing," the board found, engineers and managers increasingly regarded the foam shedding as inevitable, or an acceptable risk.

  6. The F-86 aileron bolt Aircraft production · from Yeager's autobiography

    A series of crashes traced to a bolt on the aileron cylinder installed upside down, reportedly by one worker who felt he knew better. Murphy's Law itself: if it can be done wrong, eventually it will be. Design it out.

For Mike: the four aerospace cases are as short as they are in the paper. Background from the investigation reports (and, for the F-86, Yeager's autobiography) could be added. Is that the depth you want?
