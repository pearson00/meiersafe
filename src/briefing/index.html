Discussion questions, all cases #

Each case study ends with its own questions. They are collected here so a briefing can draw on several cases at once.

The 1995 top landing

  1. Which routine operation in your program has a long record of success but has never been measured against a safety margin?
  2. When did you last think "this may not end well" during an operation, and "what could go wrong?" after it?
  3. What quantitative metric and target would tell you that a successful outcome was really luck?
  4. What does the routine save you in time, money or crew, and how does that saving shape the go decision?
  5. When someone else has a mishap doing what you do, how do you decide whether it was their technique or the operation itself?

The 2015 certification launch

  1. When did you last go because you believed it was your only chance? Was it?
  2. Who was depending on you, and would they have wanted you to go in those conditions?
  3. What would a no-go have cost you that day, and what did you tell yourself it would cost?
  4. What limit, set beforehand, would have made this a no-go without a decision in the moment?

Apollo 1

  1. Which condition in your test program has been present on every success so far, and has never been treated as a hazard?
  2. What earlier incidents, in your program or in others, point at a danger you have not yet experienced yourself?
  3. Who on your team would say "these test conditions are hazardous," and when did anyone last say it?
  4. Where has a long run of success in your program turned into overconfidence or complacency?

Challenger

  1. Which limit in your program has been widened after a run of successes, and on what evidence?
  2. What damage or anomaly do you now call "acceptable" that was once unexpected?
  3. If you set a quantitative target for that anomaly today, with a margin of safety, would your recent successes meet it?
  4. Where does the pressure to keep flying come from in your organization, and at what levels?
  5. When an engineer disagrees with a go decision, how does that disagreement reach the person making it?

Columbia

  1. Which known problem in your program violates a design requirement, but has never yet caused a failure?
  2. With each success, has your view of that problem moved toward "inevitable" or "acceptable"?
  3. What would it take for your program to treat a long history of a problem as more warning, not less?
  4. The board called for a new culture at NASA. Which part of the problem would a new culture not change in your own decision making?

The F-86 aileron bolt

  1. Which part or step in your build, maintenance or test process can be done the wrong way and still look right?
  2. Where do you rely on an instruction, rather than the design, to prevent a wrong installation?
  3. How would you find out that one experienced person was doing a step their own way, because they felt they knew better?
  4. Which of your designs or procedures could be changed so that the wrong way is impossible?
